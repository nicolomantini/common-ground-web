# What to expect when you start therapy

Walking into a first session — or logging into your first video call — is usually the hardest part. Most of the nervousness fades once you realize it's less like an interrogation and more like a slow, unhurried conversation.

## The first session is mostly questions

Your counselor isn't diagnosing you in the first ten minutes. They're getting a sense of what brought you in, what a good outcome would look like to you, and whether the two of you seem like a workable fit. You're allowed to ask them questions too — about their approach, their experience with what you're dealing with, how they usually structure sessions.

## You don't need the "right" words

A lot of people delay starting therapy because they feel like they need to explain themselves clearly first — as if there's a tidy way to summarize why they're struggling. There usually isn't, and that's fine. "I don't really know where to start" is a completely normal way to begin a first session.

## Progress isn't linear

Some sessions will feel productive. Others will feel like you went in circles. Both are part of the same process — it's rarely a straight line from problem to resolution, and a slow week doesn't mean it's not working.

## It's okay to switch counselors

If a few sessions in it doesn't feel right, that's useful information, not a failure on your part. Fit matters more than most people expect, and a counselor who's a great match for one person might not be for another.

If you're not sure where to start, browsing a few profiles and reading how each counselor describes their own approach is often more useful than any single article — including this one.
