# How to choose a counselor who's actually a fit

It's tempting to pick based on credentials alone — the longest bio, the most letters after the name. In practice, fit tends to come down to a handful of quieter things.

## Specialty matters more than seniority

A counselor with fifteen years of general experience isn't necessarily better suited to your situation than someone with three years focused specifically on, say, trauma or teens. Look for what they say they actually spend most of their time on, not just how long they've been practicing.

## Format is a real constraint, not a preference

If video sessions consistently get canceled because they don't fit your schedule, or in-person feels important to you and isn't offered, that friction adds up over months. Decide this up front rather than hoping it'll work itself out.

## Read how they describe themselves

Bios that sound like a form letter usually mean less than a couple of specific sentences about how someone actually works. Direct and structured? Warm and exploratory? Neither is better, but one will suit you more.

## The first session is a two-way interview

You're allowed to leave a first session and decide it's not a match — that's normal, not rude. Most counselors expect this and won't take it personally.

Take your time browsing profiles. The right fit is worth more than the fastest available opening.
