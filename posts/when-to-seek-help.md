# You don't need a crisis to start therapy

There's a common assumption that counseling is for emergencies — a breakup, a loss, a breakdown. In practice, most people who see a counselor regularly aren't in crisis. They're dealing with something quieter: low-grade stress that's been building for months, a relationship that's fine but not great, a nagging sense of being stuck.

## "Not bad enough yet" isn't a real threshold

There's no minimum severity requirement. If something is taking up mental space or affecting how you show up in your life or relationships, that's already a reasonable enough reason.

## Maintenance is a legitimate use of therapy

Plenty of people keep seeing a counselor periodically not because something is wrong, but because it's useful — a consistent space to think out loud with someone who isn't a friend or family member and has no stake in the outcome.

## Waiting for a crisis often means waiting longer than necessary

Problems that get addressed early are often smaller and easier to work through than the same problem six months later once it's calcified into a habit or a pattern. Earlier isn't premature.

If you've been telling yourself it's "not serious enough" to look into, that's usually not a good enough reason to wait.
